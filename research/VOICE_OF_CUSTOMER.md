## SECTION 2 — CONTRACTOR VOCABULARY (25 TERMS)

These are the nouns, verbs, and phrases contractors actually use — in forums, reviews, and industry publications. Use these in copy; avoid marketing substitutes.

| Term | Context |
|------|---------|
| **bid** | The formal price submitted to win a job ("I put in a bid") |
| **quote / estimate** | Used interchangeably with bid for smaller jobs ("send a quote," "write up an estimate") |
| **proposal** | More formal version of a quote, often for larger projects |
| **walk the job / job walk / site walk** | Physical visit to assess scope ("I walked the job this morning") |
| **scope of work** | The defined list of what's included in the bid |
| **scope creep** | When extra work gets added without a change order or new price |
| **change order** | Written authorization for work beyond original scope |
| **ate the cost** | Absorbed an expense not covered by the client ("I ate the cost of that extra day") |
| **punch list** | Final list of small items to complete before close-out |
| **draw** | Progress payment pulled at milestone ("waiting on my draw") |
| **retainage** | % held back until project completion, often 5–10% |
| **the guys / my crew / the crew** | Spanish-speaking or field workers ("I need to tell the guys") |
| **foreman** | On-site supervisor who translates or relays instructions |
| **GC** | General contractor |
| **sub / subcontractor** | Trade specialty hired under GC |
| **materials** | Always separate line from labor in bids |
| **labor rate / man-hour** | Per-hour cost of field worker |
| **overhead** | Non-billable costs factored into bid (truck, insurance, tools) |
| **close out** | Final phase of a job before final payment |
| **job costing** | Tracking actual vs. estimated cost per job |
| **AIA application** | Formal billing document for commercial jobs |
| **pay app** | Shorthand for payment application (subcontractor terminology) |
| **invoice** | Final billing document sent to client |
| **follow-up** | Post-quote outreach; most contractors skip this and lose jobs |
| **cash flow** | The #1 business stress word; comes up in almost every contractor forum thread |

## SECTION 4 — WORDS / CLAIMS THAT MAKE CONTRACTORS DISTRUST A TOOL

Based on review patterns, forum complaints, and industry sentiment:

1. **"Easy to use"** — Every software says this. Contractors have been burned by ServiceTitan's 8-month onboarding. This phrase now triggers skepticism.
2. **"All-in-one platform"** — Sounds like ServiceTitan. Associated with expensive contracts, bloat, and add-on fees not disclosed upfront.
3. **"Scales with your business"** — Translation: costs more as you grow. Contractors hear this and think "I'll outgrow the affordable tier immediately."
4. **"Streamline your workflow"** — Generic. Contractors can't picture what it means. Feels like marketing, not a real product.
5. **"Increase revenue"** — Sounds like a coaching upsell, not a tool. Contractors don't want to be sold business strategy; they want the job to get done.
6. **"No contract required"** — Implicitly flags that other tools trap you (good to note), but using it without context reads like desperation.
7. **"AI-powered"** — If it's the first thing you say. Contractors are practical; AI means nothing until you explain exactly what problem it solves.
8. **"We handle the paperwork"** — Too vague. They've heard this from QuickBooks, Buildertrend, etc. Must be specific.
9. **Free trial with credit card required** — Immediate trust killer. Forum threads frequently call this out as a scam signal.
10. **Testimonials with no trade/location** — "Sarah, General Contractor" means nothing. "Mike, HVAC, Phoenix" reads real.

## SECTION 6 — POSITIONING NOTES FOR OBRA COPY

**What makes contractors stop scrolling:**
- Specific numbers (10 hours saved, 83 days, $280B)
- Trade-specific language (not "construction professionals" — say "GCs," "subs," "your crew")
- The word "your guys" or "your crew" in relation to Spanish instructions lands immediately because it reflects daily reality
- Pain framed from their POV, not solution framing ("Still writing estimates at 10pm?" vs "Obra speeds up your workflow")

**Copy tone to use:** Direct, no fluff, tool-foreman register. Like a foreman talking to another foreman at the supply house.

**Copy tone to avoid:** SaaS startup ("seamless," "intuitive," "frictionless"), coaching ("transform your business"), or generic small-biz ("streamline operations").
